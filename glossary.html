Glossary

Plain-English definitions of the terms used in the planner and this manual.

Adjusted gross income (AGI)
Total income minus certain adjustments (Form 1040 line 11a). In the planner it equals total income. See taxes.
Asset class
A group of similar investments - domestic stocks, international stocks, bonds, municipal bonds, gold, cash. Every investment needs one. See asset classes.
Auto
The planner's standard way to pay a bill: from Savings, then Brokerage, IRA, 401(k) and finally Roth. See withdrawals and Auto.
Cash account
The account that receives Social Security and RMDs: the first Savings account with an investment, else the first Checking, else the first Brokerage.
Capital gain or loss
The difference between what an investment sold for and what it cost (its basis). Long-term gains are taxed at 0%, 15% or 20%.
COLA (cost-of-living adjustment)
The yearly increase in Social Security benefits. The planner uses the general inflation rate.
Cost basis
What you paid for an investment, plus reinvested dividends. Only the amount above the basis is a taxable gain when you sell.
Down and flat market
A market scenario that replays real 2000-2026 history starting at the worst point for your holdings. See normal and down markets.
Effective tax rate
Total tax divided by income - the average rate you pay.
Filing status
Single or married filing jointly. One tax payer files single, two file jointly; a surviving spouse files single from the year after the first death.
Full retirement age (FRA)
The age at which you get your full Social Security benefit: 67 if born in 1960 or later, between 66 and 67 for earlier births (the planner uses 66).
Growth
The change in an investment's price, in percent per year. Taxed only as a capital gain when sold from a taxable account.
IRMAA
Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount: extra Medicare Part B and Part D premiums for higher incomes, based on MAGI from two years earlier. See Medicare and IRMAA.
Life expectancy
The age you plan to live to. Year of birth plus life expectancy is the last year of that tax payer's income and benefits (YODTP1 / YODTP2).
MAGI (modified adjusted gross income)
For Medicare IRMAA, AGI plus tax-exempt interest. Shown in the clarity box 11a.1.
Marginal tax rate
The tax rate on your next dollar of income - the rate that matters for decisions about extra income such as a Roth conversion.
Milestone (year code)
A start or end year tied to an event, such as YORTP1 (tax payer 1's retirement). See plan milestones.
Mix
How new money paid into an account is split between its investments. Must total 100% per account.
Municipal (muni) bonds
Bonds from state and local governments. Their interest is free of federal income tax but counts for the Social Security worksheet and IRMAA.
Net asset value (NAV)
The total of all your accounts at the end of a year.
Normal market
A market scenario where every investment earns its own growth and yield, or its asset class's long-run average, every year.
Plan horizon
The number of years from now to the end of the plan (the last life expectancy year).
Qualified dividends
Most stock dividends; taxed at the lower capital gain rates (line 3a).
Required minimum distribution (RMD)
The amount you must withdraw each year from IRAs and 401(k)s from age 72, 73 or 75. See RMDs.
Roth bucket
A federal tax bracket - 22% or 24% - that the planner fills with Roth conversions each year, as the last step of the year. See Roth buckets.
Roth conversion
Moving money from a traditional IRA or 401(k) to a Roth account. It is taxed now, then grows and is withdrawn tax free.
Scenario
One run of the plan with one combination of retirement ages, claim ages, Roth bucket and market. Run Plan runs every scenario and stars the best down-market one; you choose which to view. See scenarios.
SECURE 2.0 Act
The 2022 law that set the current RMD start ages (73, rising to 75 for people born in 1960 or later).
Senior deduction
An extra $6,000 deduction per person 65 or older for 2025-2028, phased out at higher incomes (line 13b). See taxes.
Sequence of returns risk
The danger that bad market years early in retirement, while you are withdrawing, do lasting damage even if the market later recovers.
Social Security adjustment factor
The share of your estimated benefit you expect to be paid (85% to start, planning for a possible 15% cut).
Social Security benefits worksheet
The IRS worksheet that works out how much of your benefits is taxable (0% to 85%). See Social Security.
Standard deduction
A fixed amount of income that is not taxed: $16,100 single and $32,200 married in 2026, plus an extra amount for each person 65 or older.
Stitching
Repeating the 27-year market history so it covers plans of up to 75 years, with every asset class repeating in step.
Taxable income
AGI minus deductions (line 15) - the amount the tax brackets apply to.
Today's dollars
Amounts at today's prices. You enter amounts in today's dollars; the planner inflates them each year.
Total return
Growth plus yield - everything an investment earned in a year.
Trustee
The owner of an account: a tax payer or Joint.
Uniform Lifetime Table
The IRS table of divisors used to calculate RMDs, in the planner's RMD Table.
Yield
The income an investment pays - dividends or interest - in percent per year. In a taxable account it is taxed every year.
