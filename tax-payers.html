Tax Payers Built

A plan has one or two tax payers: you, and a spouse or partner if you file a joint return. Everything else in the plan - ages, retirement, Social Security, RMDs, Medicare, filing status - follows from this page.

The tax payer list

  • The list shows one entry per tax payer, named after the tax payer. It updates as you type the name.
  • Add Taxpayer adds the second tax payer (named "Taxpayer 2" until you rename it). It is turned off when there are already two.
  • Delete Taxpayer deletes the tax payer shown, after you confirm. It is turned off when only one is left, because a plan needs at least one.
  • With two tax payers the plan files married filing jointly; with one, single. In the projection, a surviving spouse files single from the year after the first death.
What deleting a tax payer changes Their wages and pension income are deleted. Their accounts, and every joint account, move to the other tax payer, who becomes tax payer 1. Income and expense dates that pointed at tax payer 2's milestones (for example YORTP2) move to the matching tax payer 1 milestone.

Demographics and retirement horizons

FieldWhat to enterShown under it
NameAny name. It labels the list, results and drop-downs. A blank name shows as "Taxpayer 1" or "Taxpayer 2".
Year of birthPick from 1900-2100. It sets ages, the full retirement age, the RMD start age and when Medicare starts.Current age
Life expectancyThe age you plan to live to. The plan runs to the later life expectancy year of the two tax payers.Plan horizon (years from now)
Retirement age 1, 2, 3Up to three ages to compare. Wages stop in the year you reach the retirement age; pensions start that year.Retirement year

Run Plan uses retirement age 1. The optimizer tries all three (repeated ages count once).

Choosing a life expectancy Plan for a long life: running out of money at 92 is a bigger problem than leaving some behind. Many planners use 90-95. The life expectancy year is also the YODTP1 / YODTP2 milestone, the last year of that tax payer's income and benefits.

Social Security

FieldWhat to enterShown under it
Claim age 1, 2, 3Up to three ages to start benefits. Run Plan uses claim age 1; the optimizer tries all three.Claim year
Min age, Full age, Max ageThe earliest claim age (62), your full retirement age (66 or 67) and the age after which waiting stops increasing the benefit (70). Filled in from the year of birth.
Min amount, Full amount, Max amountYour monthly benefit in today's dollars if you claim at the min, full and max ages, from your Social Security statement.Under Full and Max: the increase over the minimum, in dollars and percent

For a claim age between these ages, the planner draws a straight line between the amounts. See Social Security for how benefits are adjusted, taxed and passed to a survivor.

Editing tips

  • Ages and the year of birth are chosen with a picker: click the button at the right of the box. More about pickers.
  • A change is kept as soon as you leave the details area or switch to the other tax payer, and it turns on the Save button.
