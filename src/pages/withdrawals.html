Withdrawals and Auto

When a bill has to be paid - a living expense, the year's income tax, Medicare - the money comes out of your accounts. Choosing which account to draw from changes your taxes, so the planner follows a set order called Auto.

The Auto withdrawal order

Auto uses account types in this order, moving to the next step only when the previous ones are empty:

  1. Savings and Checking - already-taxed money; taking it costs no tax.
  2. Brokerage - only the gain on what is sold is taxed, at capital gain rates.
  3. IRA - every dollar is ordinary income.
  4. 401(k) - every dollar is ordinary income.
  5. Roth - tax free, and the longest-growing money, so it is used last.

Within a step, all accounts and investments pay in proportion to their balances - Savings and Checking accounts together. Investments that lost value this year are used last, so you don't sell what is down if something else can pay.

Each account type carries its own place in this order (its Auto withdrawal order, saved with the plan). Checking works exactly like Savings; the type is there so you can enter your accounts as they really are. HSA and 529 have no place in the order, so Auto never uses them. Plans saved by earlier versions get this order when they are opened.

Paying from a specific account

An expense can name an account in its From column instead of Auto - for example a 529 account for tuition, or an HSA for medical bills. That account pays first; if it runs short, Auto pays the rest. Taxes and Medicare are always paid by Auto. Money an expense takes from an IRA or 401(k) counts toward that account's RMD.

Withdrawals can create tax

  • Selling from a taxable account realizes a capital gain or loss: proceeds minus the average cost basis of what was sold.
  • Taking money from an IRA or 401(k) is ordinary income (lines 4a / 5a).
  • Each year's tax is paid the next year, the way a return filed in April is paid. A withdrawal that pays last year's tax is income of the year it is made, so it goes on that year's return and its tax is paid the year after. There is no back-and-forth recalculation.

The cash account

Social Security and RMDs need somewhere to land. The planner uses the first Savings or Checking account that holds an investment; if there is none, the first Brokerage. The plan cannot run without one of them. Auto spends the cash account first, so the year's bills are paid from Social Security and the RMD before anything else; at the end of the year any surplus is reinvested (below).

Surplus savings are reinvested

RMDs and Social Security land in savings even in years you don't need all of the money. So savings don't pile up in cash, step 7 of every year moves whatever each Savings or Checking account gained during the year to the first Brokerage account that holds an investment, where it is invested by that account's Mix.

  • Each Savings and Checking account keeps its start-of-year balance as a steady cash cushion for next year's bills. Set your starting balances to the cushion you want.
  • Only the year's gain moves: unspent RMDs and Social Security, income paid into the account, and its own growth and yield. An account that ended the year lower keeps what is left; nothing is moved back.
  • Moving cash adds no tax. If the account holds something other than cash, the sale can realize a capital gain, taxed like any other sale.
  • HSA, 529 and retirement accounts are never swept. Without a Brokerage account that holds an investment, the surplus stays where it is.
  • Each move shows in the cash flows as Reinvested Surplus.

Example. Savings start the year at $10,000. A $4,219 RMD lands there and Medicare premiums of $2,435 are paid from it, leaving $11,784. Step 7 moves the $1,784 gain to the brokerage, and savings start the next year at $10,000 again.

When the money runs out

If Auto cannot pay a bill because every account it may use is empty, the plan ends in that year. Every scenario that runs out shows that year as its Plan ends year, in red in the scenario list. Money in HSA or 529 accounts is not used to rescue the plan - name those accounts on an expense if you intend to spend them.
