Investments Preview

Your money is organized in three levels: the financial institutions you use, the accounts at each one, and the investments held in each account.

Screen in progress The picture is a mockup. The fields and rules are already used by the calculations.

Financial institutions

A bank, brokerage or fund company - for example Vanguard or your local bank. Account names in drop-downs and results show the institution, such as "Joint Savings (Local Bank)". Deleting an institution deletes its accounts and investments.

Investments

FieldMeaning
Name, TickerFor your reference, for example "Total Stock Market" / VTSAX.
Asset classRequired. DomStocks, IntStocks, Bonds, MuniBonds, Gold, Cash or a class you add. It supplies the growth and yield when you leave them blank, the down-market history, and the 1040 boxes for taxable accounts. See asset classes.
GrowthExpected price growth, % per year. Leave blank to use the asset class's long-run average ({{avgDomStocksGrowth}} for domestic stocks).
YieldExpected dividends or interest, % per year, reinvested. Leave blank to use the class average ({{avgDomStocksYield}} for domestic stocks).
MixHow new money paid into the account (income, Social Security, RMDs, Roth conversions) is split between its investments. The mixes in an account must add up to 100%. New investments get whatever is left.
Starting basisWhat you paid for the investment (its cost basis). Used to work out capital gains when it is sold in a taxable account. Ignored for IRA, 401(k), Roth, HSA and 529.
Starting balanceToday's value.
Growth and yield in the two markets In the normal market an investment uses its own Growth and Yield, or the class average when they are blank. In the down market every investment follows its asset class's real history instead, starting in the worst year for your holdings. See normal and down markets.

How balances change

  • Each year an investment grows by its growth rate and earns its yield on the balance at the start of the year; the yield is reinvested.
  • Deposits are split by Mix; withdrawals are taken in proportion to balance, and investments that lost value that year are sold last. The planner does not rebalance.
  • Sales from taxable accounts use the average cost: a sale of 10% of an investment uses 10% of its basis. Reinvested yields are added to the basis.

Checks before a run

  • Every investment must have an asset class, and every class you use must have complete performance history.
  • Each account's investment mixes must add up to 100%.
  • The plan needs a Savings, Checking or Brokerage account with at least one investment to receive Social Security and RMDs.
